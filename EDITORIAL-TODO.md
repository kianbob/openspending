## "How Government Contracts Actually Work" page (/how-it-works)

Write this in plain language, no jargon. Kian's audience is regular taxpayers, not policy wonks.

### Sections:

1. **The Money Flow** — Simple diagram/explanation:
   - Your paycheck → IRS → U.S. Treasury → Congress appropriates → Agencies get budgets → Contractors get paid
   - "Every dollar in these contracts started as someone's tax payment"

2. **How a Contract Gets Awarded**
   - Agency identifies a need
   - Puts out a solicitation (or doesn't — sole source)
   - Companies bid (or just one bids)
   - Contract awarded, work begins
   - "In theory, this is competitive. In practice, many of the biggest contracts go to the same handful of companies year after year."

3. **The No-Bid Problem**
   - Sole-source contracts = no competition
   - "Only one company can build this" is the excuse
   - Result: no price pressure, no accountability
   - DOD is the biggest offender

4. **Cost Overruns: The Norm, Not the Exception**
   - A $10B contract becomes $20B — and the contractor still gets paid
   - "Contract financing payments" = getting paid before the work is done
   - Progress payments, advance payments — the government pays upfront
   - "In the private sector, going 100% over budget gets you fired. In government contracting, it gets you a contract extension."

5. **The Revolving Door**
   - Pentagon officials → defense contractor lobbyists → back to Pentagon
   - The same people deciding who gets contracts later work for those companies
   - "This isn't a conspiracy theory — it's public record"

6. **The Concentration Problem**
   - Top 10 contractors get $150B+/year
   - Lockheed Martin alone: $51B (with subsidiaries)
   - "A handful of companies are more dependent on taxpayer money than most people realize"
   - Chart: Top 10 contractors as % of total contract spending

7. **What DOGE Is Trying to Fix**
   - Contract oversight, eliminating redundancy
   - Whether it's working — too early to tell
   - "Regardless of politics, the question 'are taxpayers getting value?' should always be asked"

## Tone
- Libertarian: skeptical of the system, pro-accountability
- Plain language, conversational
- Let the data do the talking
- Not partisan — waste is bipartisan
- "Your money, their contracts"
